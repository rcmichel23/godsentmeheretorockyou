I am trying to remember the first time I ever felt lonely. 

The most recent time is now. I've been feeling really lonely lately. This makes me feel guilty, because I have a lot of friends, because I have a lot of people in my life that love me. When I feel lonely, despite this, it makes me feel like I'm ungrateful, unable to be happy with what life has offered me, or that I'm selfish, unable to be satisfied with the company or love or whatever people in my life have offered me. It's a negative emotional loop I find myself often in. 

And the reality of it is, there are truths contained in all these characteristics. Yes, I can be selfish; yes, I am ungrateful; yes, I am guilty; and yes, despite it all, I am lonely. Because you will always be lonely if you can't find a friend in yourself right. 

It feels sort of juvenile but there is a line in Dr. Suess' book *Oh The Places You'll Go* that has haunted my mind quite a bit lately: "All alone! Whether you like it or not, alone is something you'll be quite a lot."  I remember having this book read to me as a kid and finding it really freaky. The concept of loneliness felt like some foreign toxin to the human condition, that it was something that happened to adults, or happened unto them. In a way, this idea still rings true. It does feel like some cancerous condition, avoidable if you play your social cards right or find fulfillment within some part of your interior. The book is find of freaky from what I recall. Psychedelic images, antithetical wanderlust, I guess it ends on some ambiguous high note about finding your way but like I don't know I just looked up the ending to make sure what I was writing was correct and it kind of made me feel more depressed. 

But yeah, I don't remember the first time I experienced loneliness, but when I moved from Las Vegas back to New Orleans when I was 13, I began experience intense bouts loneliness and it never really went away. The thing about being a teenager is you really haven't lived at all, even if you think you have, so I had no other frame of reference for how life could be. I, like a lot of my generation, spent a lot of my high school years on tumblr and twitter and my digital company often felt more involved than the company I kept irl. Tale as old as time whatever.
